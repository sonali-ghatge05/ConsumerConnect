A valid consumer dispute arises when there is a defect in goods purchased or a deficiency in services hired/availed. This includes situations involving unfair trade practices, misleading advertisements, charging above the MRP, or selling hazardous goods without proper warnings.

No, engaging a lawyer is not mandatory. The consumer protection framework is designed to be accessible, allowing individuals to file complaints and represent themselves. However, for complex cases involving substantial claims, seeking legal advice might be beneficial.

Yes, a nominal fee is usually required, which varies depending on the value of goods or services and the compensation claimed. However, for claims up to a certain threshold (e.g., ₹5 Lakhs under current provisions), no fee is charged to make the process more accessible for smaller disputes.

The Consumer Protection Act mandates that disputes should be resolved within 3 months if no product analysis is required, and 5 months if laboratory testing is necessary. However, actual timelines may vary based on case complexity and commission backlog.

Yes, the e-Daakhil portal allows consumers to easily file complaints electronically from anywhere. This portal integrates with the National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC) and state/district commissions, streamlining the initial filing process.
